# Related Work on Prediction Depth

**English** · [简体中文](../i18n/zh-CN/docs/literature.md) · [日本語](../i18n/ja/docs/literature.md) · [Esperanto](../i18n/eo/docs/literature.md)

This focused review compares the current results with work on parallel prediction of additive and quasidirect-product automata, bidiagonal powers and binomial parity, Boolean monomial dynamics, whole-state freezing, and circuit complexity. The comparisons distinguish input encodings, gate bases, uniformity, and rule classes.

The paper gives the full statements, proof outlines, and citations. [English PDF](../paper/structural-certificates.pdf) · [Typst source](../paper/structural-certificates.typ)

## Structural certificates

- **A, composable representations.** Ladner and Fischer construct parallel prefix circuits for associative operations. Moore composes time-interval transfer maps for additive cellular automata. Certificate A checks the obligations for a chosen representation of iterates, including the encodings reached by binary powering, the costs of executed circuit operations, and, when required, uniform generation. [Ladner and Fischer (1980)](https://doi.org/10.1145/322217.322232) · [Moore, §3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/patt-sol/9701008#page=7) · [Encoded path certificate](../PredictionDepth/EncodedPathComposition.lean) · [Uniform composition](../PredictionDepth/Complexity/UniformComposition.lean)
- **B, simulation and reduction.** Hardness transfer follows the classical reduction method. The certificate specifies the evidence a concrete rule must supply, including a logspace generator for the full unary-time query and equality of the simulated output and the observation. P membership and the separation needed for uniform NC exclusion are separate hypotheses. [Hardness and its hypotheses](hardness.md)
- **C, essential-input dependence.** At depth $`d`$, a fan-in-two output can depend on at most $`2^d`$ coordinates. The certificate supplies a pair of inputs that differ only at the relevant coordinate and yield different outputs, for every coordinate counted. With a fixed number of bits per site and a polynomial-volume light cone, the numerical lower bound from this count grows at most logarithmically in time. The actual circuit depth may be larger. [Dependency witnesses](../PredictionDepth/Circuit.lean) · [Counting bound](../PredictionDepth/DependencyGrowth.lean)

## Comparison baseline

Fix the control-word width $`q`$ and stay and hop guards $`s,h`$. The input explicitly supplies the control words and data bits at sites $`0,\ldots,T`$, with legal length $`n=(q+1)(T+1)`$. The output is the data bit at the origin after $`T`$ steps. The upper bounds use De Morgan gates of fan-in at most two, with arbitrary wiring and sharing. One deterministic logspace transducer emits the complete circuit from $`1^n`$. The essential-input lower bounds cover any Boolean gate basis of fan-in at most two. In the De Morgan model a NOT gate counts as one layer and an input wire has depth zero. [Model and encoding](frozen-pascal.md) · [Definition of uniformity](../PredictionDepth/Complexity/UniformNC.lean)

The exact De Morgan depth in the homogeneous case is a consequence of Khrapchenko's parity formula bound. For $`p>0`$, parity of $`2^p`$ bits needs at least $`2^{2p}`$ leaves after unfolding a circuit into a formula and pushing negations to the leaves. Parity is nonmonotone, so the original formula contains a unary NOT. A depth-$`2p`$ tree of fan-in at most two with such a node has strictly fewer than $`2^{2p}`$ leaves. Conversely, complementing the inputs and computing parity and its complement together takes one NOT layer and two AND/OR layers per level of a balanced tree. The minimum is therefore $`2p+1`$ for $`p>0`$ and zero for $`p=0`$. With XOR as a binary gate it is exactly $`p`$. At successive times $`2^r-1`$ and $`2^r`$, the De Morgan depth drops from $`2r+1`$ to three for $`r\ge2`$. The existing uniform generator's bound is $`3p`$. [Khrapchenko (1971)](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/mzm9639) · [Uniform generator](../PredictionDepth/FrozenPascal/UniformHomogeneous.lean)

## Transported controls

Transported control is also a quasidirect-product special case. At site $`i`$, the control track reads the old control bit at $`i+u`$. The data track multiplies the old data bit at $`i+v`$ by the old control bit at $`i`$, with $`u,v\in\{0,1\}`$. Moore's general framework permits such time-varying coefficients. A single backwards data path counts the distinct initial control bits and gives the exact fan-in-two depth for each time. At positive times equal velocities read the same initial control bit repeatedly, giving depth one. Unequal velocities read $`T`$ different initial control bits, giving depth $`\lceil\log_2(T+1)\rceil`$. A moving control track can therefore force logarithmic depth even when the data stays in place. [Moore, §3, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/patt-sol/9701008) · [Lean exact-depth theorem](../PredictionDepth/TransportedControl.lean)

For finite Boolean monomial systems, Colón-Reyes, Laubenbacher, and Pareigis identify the variables of a nonzero iterate with endpoints of fixed-length dependency walks. This applies on the finite light cone of the transported rule, whose coordinates are conjunctions. The present comparison calculates the size of that support explicitly in terms of the two velocities and uses it to give the exact depth. [Colón-Reyes et al., Proposition 2.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0403166#page=4)

The branching rule with $`c'_i=c_i\oplus c_{i+1}`$ and $`x'_i=c_ix_i\oplus x_{i+1}`$ falls under Moore's Examples 1 and 3. Its data update chooses a pure shift or a Pascal step according to the current control. For this rule the paper gives a specialized transfer formula using one shared Pascal parity and tests for all-zero suffixes, with matching logarithmic depth bounds. The transfer coefficient can distinguish controls with the same frozen prefix counts. [Moore, §3, Examples 1 and 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/patt-sol/9701008#page=9) · [Paper](../paper/structural-certificates.pdf)

## Sparse corrections and forced response

- Moore's additive decomposition includes non-autonomous linear evolution and a specific defect example. The residual here is defined by comparing two updates on the same actual predecessor state. The complete-cone check makes the accepting residual unique, even when one correction causes another. [Moore, §3 and §4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/patt-sol/9701008) · [Sparse correction proofs](../paper/structural-certificates.pdf)
- Known-position enumeration is related to Williams, Gomes, and Selman's distinction between exploiting and finding backdoors. Chronological repair instead selects the earliest mismatch by a parallel prefix computation. The residual weight is a property of the queried trajectory. Static low-depth circuit synthesis and freezing constrain different objects. [Williams et al. (2003)](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2003/167) · [Ladner and Fischer (1980)](https://doi.org/10.1145/322217.322232) · [Boyar et al. (2019)](https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=924490) · [Ollinger and Theyssier (2022)](https://doi.org/10.46298/dmtcs.5734)
- Rowland and Yassawi establish automaticity results for scalar linear cellular-automaton space-time diagrams. The paper uses section operators on a rational matrix generating function to read coefficients for fixed multitrack rules, then combines those coefficients with the rectangular forced-response formula to generate the entire circuit. The coefficient method uses established finite-field power series techniques. [Rowland and Yassawi (2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.01256) · [Adamczewski et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02640)

## Findings and scope

- The classification covers all fixed guard pairs $`s,h`$. No-hop and pure-shift rules have uniform constant depth. The homogeneous Pascal profile depends on the number of set bits. The remaining rules have matching logarithmic bounds. Lean verifies the executable classifier and one family's correctness, depth, size, and uniformity across all lengths. [Classifier](../PredictionDepth/FrozenPascal/Classification.lean) · [Uniform classification](../PredictionDepth/FrozenPascal/UniformClassification.lean)
- The essential-input lower bound applies with bounded fan-in. For $`q=1, s\equiv0, h(a)=a`$, prediction is the conjunction of the furthest initial data bit and the intervening controls. One unbounded-fan-in AND gate suffices. The explicit initial input has length linear in $`T`$. A succinct state with a binary time changes the complexity parameters.
- The specific claims for comparison are the four-way finite guard test, the matching logarithmic bounds in the varying-guard branch, and a single Lean-verified logspace-uniform realization of the branch bounds. This focused review leaves priority for their combination open. The transported example provides a separate test of how changing the relative velocity changes the essential inputs.
